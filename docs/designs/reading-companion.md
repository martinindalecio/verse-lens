## Problem Statement

While reading Acts, Martin had "trouble connecting the dots between Saul of Tarsus, the eunuch, the other Saul, and similar figures." The text assumes you track who is who across chapters and books: Saul becomes Paul at Acts 13:9 without fanfare, King Saul is mentioned in Acts 13:21, the Philip in Acts 8 is the evangelist (Acts 6) and not the apostle, and there are several Herods, Jameses, Johns and Marys.

The app has to answer two questions while you read, each one tap away from the text:

1. **"Who is this?"**: tap a name, get a person card.
2. **"What's happening here?"**: tap a verse, get the passage explanation (exists today).

## What Makes This Cool

- Tap "Saul" in Acts 9 and see *Saul of Tarsus, later called Paul*, what he's done **up to where you are** (present at Stephen's stoning, Acts 7:58; persecuting the church, 8:3) and who he's connected to (Barnabas, Ananias). Tap Barnabas and his card opens in the same sheet.
- Identities come from scholarly data, so the app never confuses the two Philips. Even the AI explanation gets told which Philip it's looking at.
- The same app on your iPhone (installed to the home screen) and laptop, picking up where you stopped.

## Constraints

- Personal tool first. Must be pleasant enough to use daily, one-handed on an iPhone.
- iPhone and laptop. Web app installable to the iPhone home screen; no App Store for now.
- Bible text: public-domain BSB (already bundled). People data: STEPBible TIPNR, **CC BY 4.0** (credit required). Places/events later: Theographic, **CC BY-SA 4.0** (anything published from it must carry the same license, so keep it in separate tables/files).
- AI cost has to stay flat as usage grows: generate once per passage or person, store it, serve it to everyone.

## Premises

1. Two questions drive the app equally: "Who is this?" (person card) and "What's happening here?" (passage explanation). Maps, translation comparison and imagery come after both work well.
2. Who someone *is* comes from scholarly data, never from AI. AI adds "why they matter", relationships the data lacks, and episode summaries, all labeled as AI with a basis and confidence.
3. "Story so far" is relative to where you're reading. Tapping Paul in Acts 9 shows Acts 7-9. A "Full profile" link shows everything.
4. One web app for iPhone and laptop, installable to the iPhone home screen. Native app is a later option.
5. Using two devices means syncing your reading position, which needs sign-in. That also unlocks private notes and highlights later.
6. The card must work one-handed: a bottom sheet on phones that's readable in about 5 seconds, then you're back in the text.

## Approaches Considered

- **A. Data-first cards, no sign-in.** Fastest route to fixing the Acts problem. Not chosen: Martin wants sync and the full companion from the start.
- **B. Full reading companion.** **Chosen.** Detailed below.
- **C. "Who's here" cast strip + "Previously…" + read-one-person's-story mode.** Kept as a later addition on top of B (phase 6), since it solves the "catching up" use case well.

## Recommended Approach: B, built in phases

### Use cases

| # | Use case | Where it lives | Phase |

| 2 | **Understand a passage**: tap a verse, get its context | Passage tab (exists), now aware of who's who | 2, 4 |
| 3 | **Switch devices**: stop on iPhone, continue on laptop | Reading position sync | 3 |
| 4 | **Look someone up**: "who was Apollos?" | Search → person card / profile | 5 |

### UX

**One sheet, two tabs: Passage | People.**

- Phone: bottom sheet with two heights. *Peek* (~40% of the screen, the text stays visible above it) and *full* (drag up). Swipe down to close.
- Laptop: the same content in the right-hand panel that exists today.
- In the text, people's names get a subtle dotted underline. **Tap a name → People tab with that person's card. Tap elsewhere on a verse → selects it for the Passage tab.**

**Person card, top to bottom:**

1. **Header:** name, short identifier ("of Tarsus", "the evangelist"), and the alias line *"Also called Paul (from Acts 13:9)"*. Shows one AI one-liner on why they matter, labeled.
2. **So far:** episodes this person has been in *before the verse you're reading*, newest first, three visible plus "show earlier". Each line is a one-sentence episode summary with its reference: *"Guards the cloaks at Stephen's stoning, Acts 7:58"*. Tapping the reference jumps to it in the text (with a "back to Acts 9:4" chip so you never lose your place).
3. **Connected to:** chips of related people, each with the relationship on it: *Barnabas, vouched for him (Acts 9:27)*, *Ananias, restored his sight*. Family relationships come from TIPNR and are shown as fact. Other relationships are AI-generated and marked as such. Tapping a chip opens that person's card **inside the sheet**, with a back arrow (a small navigation stack), so you can follow a chain of people and come back.
4. **Footer:** "Full profile →" and the data credit (STEPBible TIPNR, CC BY 4.0).

Card connections to the Passage tab: the passage explanation lists people in the passage as chips that open their cards, and the person card's episode references open the passage.

### Data pipeline (build time, a script like `scripts/build-bible.py`)

1. Download TIPNR from STEPBible-Data. Parse people: id, names, description, family relations, and every verse reference.
2. **Tag names in the BSB text.** For each (person, verse) pair, find the person's English name or alias in that verse's BSB text and record a mention `{book, chapter, verse, start, end, personId}`. Write every miss or double match to a review file, to be fixed by hand or by a small overrides file.
3. Group each person's mentions into **episodes** (consecutive verses/chapters).
4. Load everything into Postgres.

### Storage (Supabase: Postgres + Auth)

- Reference data: `people`, `person_names` (aliases), `person_mentions`, `episodes`, `relationships` (`source: 'data' | 'ai'`, `basis`, `confidence`).
- Saved AI results: `passage_context` (replaces the in-memory cache), `person_enrichment` (one-liner, AI relationships, episode summaries), each with `prompt_version`.
- Per user: `reading_positions` (book, chapter, verse, device, updated_at); `notes` and `highlights` later.
- Bible text stays as bundled JSON (fast, static, no reason to move it).
- Row-level security: reference data readable by everyone; user tables only by their owner.

### AI

- **Person enrichment**, generated once per person on first view and stored: why they matter, relationships beyond family (each with the verse it rests on), and a one-sentence summary per episode. Same rules as the context panel: basis plus confidence, never invent a citation.
- **Passage context** gets a "people in this passage" list from the data (*"Philip = Philip the evangelist, Acts 6:5"*) so explanations use the right identities.
- Optional pre-generation of Acts and the Gospels with the Batch API at half price.

### Sign-in and sync

- Supabase Auth with an emailed magic link (no password). Sign in with Apple can come later if it matters.
- The reader saves your position while you scroll, a few seconds after you stop. Opening the app offers "Continue at Acts 9:4 (from iPhone, 2 h ago)".
- The app stays fully usable signed out; sign-in only adds sync.

### Installable on iPhone

- Next.js web manifest + icons (`app/manifest.ts`, see `node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/02-guides/progressive-web-apps.md`), so "Add to Home Screen" opens it full-screen like an app.
- Bible text and people data cached for offline reading; AI features need a connection.

### Build order

1. **Data:** TIPNR import, name tagging with review report, episodes, Supabase schema and load.
2. **Person card + unified sheet:** tappable names, card with so-far (references only, no AI yet) and family relationships, card-to-card navigation, phone bottom sheet. *First version you can read Acts with.*
3. **Sign-in + sync:** magic link, reading positions, "continue where you left off".
4. **AI enrichment:** person one-liners, relationships, episode summaries; passage context gets people data and moves to the database cache.
5. **Install + reach:** web manifest, offline caching, search, full profile pages, shareable links.
6. **Approach C:** chapter cast strip and "Previously…".
7. **Later:** maps (Theographic places), translation comparison, imagery.

## Open Questions

- **How accurate is automatic name tagging?** Unknown until the script runs on Acts. Measure misses and double matches before building the UI around it.
- **Does TIPNR's family data cover enough** for useful "Connected to" chips without AI? Check after import.
- **Where should episode summaries come from**: AI per episode, or simpler section headings? Try AI on Acts and judge.
- **Rate limiting and cost caps** for AI calls, now that results are stored but first views still cost money. Probably limit generation to signed-in users.
- **Theographic share-alike:** fine for a personal app; revisit before publishing derived data.

## Success Criteria

- Reading Acts 6-13 on the iPhone, every Saul, Philip, Herod, James and John resolves to the right person, with zero confusions on Martin's own test list (see Assignment).
- The person card opens in under 300 ms and is readable in about 5 seconds, one-handed.
- Stop reading on the iPhone, open the laptop, and land on the same verse.
- No identity in the app comes from AI. Everything AI-generated is labeled.

## Distribution Plan

Web app deployed on Vercel (or similar), installed to the iPhone home screen. Deploys automatically on push to `main`. No App Store.

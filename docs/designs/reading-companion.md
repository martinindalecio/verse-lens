## Revision 2 (after UX research, 2026-10-31)

Findings and reasoning: `docs/research/2026-10-ux-research.md`. These decisions override anything below that conflicts with them.

- **All 12 proposed design changes accepted** (whole-verse tap and ranges, one back history, real iOS-style sheet, identity-first card, spoiler-safe "so far" with a show-everything switch, family trees, three description lengths, plain AI labels plus "Report a mistake", text-size support, safe areas and dark mode, every state designed, Spanish-ready structure). Full list in the research doc.
- **Divine names are tappable**: God, Jesus, the Holy Spirit and "the Lord" get cards like other people, with a lighter underline than other names because they appear in nearly every verse. Their "so far" needs its own design (a timeline for God makes little sense); Jesus can use the normal card.
- **Audience widened**: personal first, then friends and a church community. That raises the bar on accessibility (older readers, large text), trust labels and cost limits.
- **Spanish**: plan for every freely licensed Spanish text: Versión Biblia Libre (CC BY-SA), Reina-Valera 1909 (public domain), La Biblia en Español Sencillo (CC BY), Palabra de Dios para ti (CC BY-SA), Nueva Biblia Viva (CC BY-SA) and La Biblia Libre para el Mundo (public domain). Copyrighted ones (RVR1960, NVI, LBLA/NBLA, TLA) only with a license. Person names, stored AI text and URLs are keyed by language and translation from day one.
- **Visual identity (decided 2026-11-05)**: Wayfinder (Atkinson Hyperlegible throughout, large tap targets, the story drawn as a route with "You are here") plus Stained glass's per-person colors for names, assigned per chapter so they stay distinct. Mockups: the "Three visual directions" row of the design canvas.
- **Scope (decided 2026-11-05)**: full approach B, not an MVP cut. Build order unchanged.
- **Database: Neon, not Supabase (decided 2026-11-05)**. Both free Supabase slots are taken by other projects and pausing them was ruled out. Neon is open-source hosted Postgres with up to 100 free projects and its own auth (Neon Auth), so the schema is unchanged and could move to Supabase later. Neon has no file storage or mobile SDKs, so web, iOS and later Android all go through the app's API routes; clients never hold database credentials. Schema: `db/schema.sql`. Connection string lives in the environment secret `DATABASE_URL`, never in the repo. Native iOS/Android apps are a later decision; the API-first setup keeps them possible. **Driver (2026-11-08):** `@neondatabase/serverless` over HTTPS (`src/lib/db.ts`), using the pooled `DATABASE_URL`; loads use `DATABASE_URL_UNPOOLED` (`scripts/load-people.mjs`). Neon's guide prefers `pg` with Vercel Fluid compute, but the HTTP driver also runs on Vercel and is the only transport usable from the cloud dev sandbox (no direct Postgres TCP there). Revisit if queries become latency-sensitive: a person card makes ~7 round trips.
- **Name tagging, phase 1 results** (`docs/research/people-tagging.md`, `scripts/build-people.py`): 91% of TIPNR's person references are found in the BSB text; in Acts every name actually printed is found, apart from 12 in chapters with two same-named people (two Johns in Acts 4, two Simons in Acts 10, two Sauls in Acts 13), which need a hand-checked overrides file. Words for peoples ("Jews", "Levites") are not tagged as the ancestor. Names the BSB adds for clarity are filled from chapter context and marked as inferred.
- **TIPNR's descriptions are AI-written** (Claude 3 Opus, April 2024, adapted by STEPBible). Identity (IDs, verse references, family) is scholarly data; the brief/short/article descriptions get the same "written by AI" label as our own text.
- **Divine names are rule-based**: TIPNR does not list every verse for God or the LORD and has no Holy Spirit entry, so "God", "the LORD", "the Lord" and "the Holy Spirit" are tagged by text rules, not data. "Lord" can also mean "sir" or "master", so those rules need review. Jesus has a full TIPNR entry.
- **Skills in the repo** (`.claude/skills/`, see `SOURCES.md`): Nielsen heuristics audit, cognitive walkthrough and WCAG audit for reviews; usability test plan, test scenarios and interview summary for testing; `frontend-design` for visual polish.
- **Real-user testing**: run `docs/research/usability-test.md` with 5 people on the mockup, then again on the prototype.

## Revision 3 (autoplan review, 2026-11-21)

These decisions override anything below that conflicts with them. Review record: `/autoplan` (CEO, DX and Eng passes, condensed), 2026-11-21.

**Order:** passage context per chapter lands first, because the cap tests and the report target format depend on its key.

- **Passage context per chapter (decided 2026-11-22).** Readers still tap and select verses; the stored explanation covers the whole chapter and the client highlights the selected verses. Chapters over 30 verses (`MAX_VERSES`) are split into fixed consecutive parts of at most 30 verses (`data/bsb` has no section headings); the key is (lang, translation, book, chapter, part, prompt_version). The prompt no longer marks a selection with `>>`. Bump `PROMPT_VERSION` and migrate the `passage_context` key. This bounds cost by the number of chapter parts and makes Batch API pre-generation possible (TODOS).
- **Daily generation cap** (before any production deploy and before phase 4). Table `generation_days (day date primary key, n int not null)`. The check runs inside `loadOrGenerate` after the cache miss and the in-flight join (`src/lib/context.ts`), never in the route, so cached and joined requests spend nothing. Query (day = current UTC date, injectable in tests): `insert into generation_days (day, n) select $1::date, 1 where $2::int > 0 on conflict (day) do update set n = generation_days.n + 1 where generation_days.n < $2::int returning n`; no row returned means the cap is reached. `$2` is `DAILY_GENERATION_CAP` (non-negative integer; missing or invalid → 200; 0 turns generation off). The slot is spent before the call, so refused or failed calls count; the client shows "try later" instead of retrying on its own. If the counter query fails, no model call is made and the route returns `upstream_failed` (502, the same status as other upstream failures). Over the cap, `/api/v1/context` and later person enrichment return the new `ApiErrorCode` `daily_cap_reached` (HTTP 429, `Retry-After` = seconds to the next UTC midnight) with "Today's limit for new explanations is reached; saved ones still work." Two instances missing the cache for the same chapter part both spend a slot; `on conflict do nothing` keeps one row. The per-IP limiter is unchanged (shared limiter stays TODOS #1).
- **Deployments until the cap ships.** Production deployments stay off (production branch changed in the Vercel dashboard; `git.deploymentEnabled: {main: false}` would also stop previews). Preview deployments require Vercel Authentication, or have no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, because otherwise anyone with a preview URL spends without a cap. Sharing the production URL with friends or a church group also waits for TODOS #1.
- **Tests and CI.** Vitest as a dev dependency (`"test": "vitest run"`, with the `@/` alias resolved), `"typecheck": "tsc --noEmit"`, `"test:identity": "node scripts/check-identity.mjs"`. Database tests run against a per-run Neon branch (or truncate between tests) and skip cleanly when `DATABASE_URL_TEST` is unset; model calls are always mocked. `.github/workflows/test.yml` runs typecheck and tests on pull requests and pushes to `main`. Cap tests: (a) the (cap+1)th new chapter part gets 429 `daily_cap_reached` with `Retry-After` in (0, 86400] while a cached one returns 200 and the counter does not move; (b) 10 concurrent distinct parts at cap-3 make exactly 3 model calls; (c) cap 0 refuses all, and "abc", "-1" or missing mean 200; (d) a failing counter query returns 502 with no model call; (e) two concurrent requests for the same part spend 1 slot and make 1 model call.
- **Identity test set** (now). `data/people/identity-tests.json` lists (book, chapter, verse, form, expected person id). `scripts/check-identity.mjs` checks it against `data/people/mentions/`: a case passes when a mention in that verse whose text slice (character offsets into the BSB verse) equals the form has the expected id; a wrong id exits 1. Seed: the plan's named cases (Philip the evangelist vs the apostle in Acts 6 and 8, Saul/Paul at Acts 13:9, King Saul at 13:21, the Herods, Jameses, Johns and Marys in Acts). Success criterion 1 is met only after Martin's Acts 6-13 list is added and passes. CI runs it when `data/people/**` or `scripts/build-people.py` change.
- **In-app mistake reports** (phase 2 fix, after the chapter key). `POST /api/v1/reports` with `target` `person:<id>` (must exist in `people`) or `passage:<book>.<chapter>.<part>` (must be a real chapter part), `message` 1-2000 characters (stored as text, never rendered as HTML, and never given to a model without being wrapped as untrusted data), `lang` from the supported list, `user_id` null until phase 3 and then from the session only (a `user_id` in the body is ignored), 5 per minute per IP plus a daily global insert cap like `generation_days`. Stores to `mistake_reports`; works signed out. The person card and the Passage tab get an inline form with sent and failed states; the GitHub link goes away. `docs/operations.md` holds the SQL to read open reports.
- **Person card speed** (phase 2 fix). Target, used everywhere the plan says "under 300 ms": server-side p95 under 300 ms with Vercel and Neon in the same region (named in `docs/operations.md`), measured by `scripts/time-card.mjs` over about 20 different people and positions with the cache cold, read from a `Server-Timing` header that every `/api/v1` route sends. Changes in `src/lib/people.ts`: stop the alias loop from walking every mention when a name form never appears in the BSB (count only forms present in the BSB, or read first appearances in SQL); load each needed chapter once with `Promise.all`; filter the "not to be confused with" query by translation and precompute a mention count on `people`. A per-person cache, if needed, holds reference data only in a bounded LRU keyed by a data version that `load-people` writes; AI text (`person_texts`) is always read live. This replaces the old TODOS item "Faster person cards".
- **Phase 3 sign-in and sync.** Authorization lives in the API routes: the user id comes from the session only. Cookies are SameSite=Lax and writes check `Origin`. `reading_positions` gets a random per-install `device_id` plus a display label, and a `client_seq`; the upsert applies only when the incoming `client_seq` is higher and sets `updated_at = now()`. The app saves nothing until the reader scrolls or accepts "Continue at…". Tests: signed out → 401; user A's write with B's id in the body writes A's row; a GET as A never returns B's rows; a write with a foreign `Origin` → 403; a lower `client_seq` is a no-op. Confirm whether Neon Auth sends magic links or one-time codes before starting.
- **API contract before native clients** (DX). One way to write a passage reference in every route and report target, one response wrapper, field-level `details` on 400s, `Retry-After` on every 429, a JSON 404 for unknown `/api/v1` paths, and the rule "clients treat unknown error codes as generic". `/api/v1/schema` serves OpenAPI generated from Zod schemas for every response; `docs/api.md` has a curl example per route. README gets prerequisites (Node ≥ 20.11, Neon account and CLI), `npm run setup`, `engines` / `.nvmrc`, and "open /read/acts/9 and tap Saul" (person cards need no Anthropic key).

## Problem Statement

While reading Acts, Martin had "trouble connecting the dots between Saul of Tarsus, the eunuch, the other Saul, and similar figures." The text assumes you track who is who across chapters and books: Saul becomes Paul at Acts 13:9 without fanfare, King Saul is mentioned in Acts 13:21, the Philip in Acts 8 is the evangelist (Acts 6) and not the apostle, and there are several Herods, Jameses, Johns and Marys.

The app has to answer two questions while you read, each one tap away from the text:

1. **"Who is this?"**: tap a name, get a person card.
2. **"What's happening here?"**: tap a verse, get the passage explanation (exists today).

## What Makes This Cool

- Tap "Saul" in Acts 9 and see *Saul of Tarsus, later called Paul*, what he's done **up to where you are** (present at Stephen's stoning, Acts 7:58; persecuting the church, 8:3) and who he's connected to (Barnabas, Ananias). Tap Barnabas and his card opens in the same sheet.
- Identities come from scholarly data, so the app never confuses the two Philips. Even the AI explanation gets told which Philip it's looking at.
- The same app on your iPhone (installed to the home screen) and laptop, picking up where you stopped.

## Constraints

- Personal tool first. Must be pleasant enough to use daily, one-handed on an iPhone.
- iPhone and laptop. Web app installable to the iPhone home screen; no App Store for now.
- Bible text: public-domain BSB (already bundled). People data: STEPBible TIPNR, **CC BY 4.0** (credit required). Places/events later: Theographic, **CC BY-SA 4.0** (anything published from it must carry the same license, so keep it in separate tables/files).
- AI cost has to stay flat as usage grows: generate once per passage or person, store it, serve it to everyone.

## Premises

1. Two questions drive the app equally: "Who is this?" (person card) and "What's happening here?" (passage explanation). Maps, translation comparison and imagery come after both work well.
2. Who someone *is* comes from scholarly data, never from AI. AI adds "why they matter", relationships the data lacks, and episode summaries, all labeled as AI with a basis and confidence.
3. "Story so far" is relative to where you're reading. Tapping Paul in Acts 9 shows Acts 7-9. A "Full profile" link shows everything.
4. One web app for iPhone and laptop, installable to the iPhone home screen. Native app is a later option.
5. Using two devices means syncing your reading position, which needs sign-in. That also unlocks private notes and highlights later.
6. The card must work one-handed: a bottom sheet on phones that's readable in about 5 seconds, then you're back in the text.

## Approaches Considered

- **A. Data-first cards, no sign-in.** Fastest route to fixing the Acts problem. Not chosen: Martin wants sync and the full companion from the start.
- **B. Full reading companion.** **Chosen.** Detailed below.
- **C. "Who's here" cast strip + "Previously…" + read-one-person's-story mode.** Kept as a later addition on top of B (phase 6), since it solves the "catching up" use case well.

### UX

**One sheet, two tabs: Passage | People.**

- Phone: bottom sheet with two heights. *Peek* (~40% of the screen, the text stays visible above it) and *full* (drag up). Swipe down to close.
- Laptop: the same content in the right-hand panel that exists today.
- In the text, people's names get a subtle dotted underline. **Tap a name → People tab with that person's card. Tap elsewhere on a verse → selects it for the Passage tab.**

**Person card, top to bottom:**

1. **Header:** name, short identifier ("of Tarsus", "the evangelist"), and the alias line *"Also called Paul (from Acts 13:9)"*. Shows one AI one-liner on why they matter, labeled.
2. **So far:** episodes this person has been in *before the verse you're reading*, newest first, three visible plus "show earlier". Each line is a one-sentence episode summary with its reference: *"Guards the cloaks at Stephen's stoning, Acts 7:58"*. Tapping the reference jumps to it in the text (with a "back to Acts 9:4" chip so you never lose your place).
3. **Connected to:** chips of related people, each with the relationship on it: *Barnabas, vouched for him (Acts 9:27)*, *Ananias, restored his sight*. Family relationships come from TIPNR and are shown as fact. Other relationships are AI-generated and marked as such. Tapping a chip opens that person's card **inside the sheet**, with a back arrow (a small navigation stack), so you can follow a chain of people and come back.
4. **Footer:** "Full profile →" and the data credit (STEPBible TIPNR, CC BY 4.0).

Card connections to the Passage tab: the passage explanation lists people in the passage as chips that open their cards, and the person card's episode references open the passage.

### Data pipeline (build time, a script like `scripts/build-bible.py`)

1. Download TIPNR from STEPBible-Data. Parse people: id, names, description, family relations, and every verse reference.
2. **Tag names in the BSB text.** For each (person, verse) pair, find the person's English name or alias in that verse's BSB text and record a mention `{book, chapter, verse, start, end, personId}`. Write every miss or double match to a review file, to be fixed by hand or by a small overrides file.
3. Group each person's mentions into **episodes** (consecutive verses/chapters).
4. Load everything into Postgres.

### Storage (Neon Postgres; auth via Neon Auth)

- Reference data: `people`, `person_names` (aliases), `person_mentions`, `episodes`, `relationships` (`source: 'data' | 'ai'`, `basis`, `confidence`).
- Saved AI results: `passage_context` (done: `src/lib/context.ts`), `person_texts` (one-liner, episode summaries) and `ai_relationships`, each with `prompt_version`.
- Per user: `reading_positions` (book, chapter, verse, device, updated_at); `notes` and `highlights` later.
- Bible text stays as bundled JSON (fast, static, no reason to move it).
- Authorization lives in the API routes (clients never touch the database): reference data is public; every per-user query filters on the signed-in user's id.

### AI

- **Person enrichment**, generated once per person on first view and stored: why they matter, relationships beyond family (each with the verse it rests on), and a one-sentence summary per episode. Same rules as the context panel: basis plus confidence, never invent a citation.
- **Passage context** gets a "people in this passage" list from the data (*"Philip = Philip the evangelist, Acts 6:5"*) so explanations use the right identities.
- Pre-generation of Acts and the Gospels with the Batch API at half price: in TODOS.md, unblocked now that passage context is keyed per chapter part.

### Sign-in and sync

- Neon Auth with an emailed magic link (no password); if Neon Auth only offers an emailed one-time code, use that. Confirm before phase 3. Sign in with Apple can come later if it matters.
- The reader saves your position while you scroll, a few seconds after you stop. Opening the app offers "Continue at Acts 9:4 (from iPhone, 2 h ago)".
- The app stays fully usable signed out; sign-in only adds sync.

### Installable on iPhone

- Next.js web manifest + icons (`app/manifest.ts`, see `node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/02-guides/progressive-web-apps.md`), so "Add to Home Screen" opens it full-screen like an app.
- Bible text and people data cached for offline reading; AI features need a connection.

### Build order

1. **Data:** TIPNR import, name tagging with review report, episodes, Neon schema and load.
2. **Person card + unified sheet:** tappable names, card with so-far (references only, no AI yet) and family relationships, card-to-card navigation, phone bottom sheet. *First version you can read Acts with.*
3. **Sign-in + sync:** magic link, reading positions, "continue where you left off".
4. **AI enrichment:** person one-liners, relationships, episode summaries; passage context gets people data (its database cache is already done).
5. **Install + reach:** web manifest, offline caching, search, full profile pages, shareable links.
6. **Approach C:** chapter cast strip and "Previously…".
7. **Later:** maps (Theographic places), translation comparison, imagery.

## Open Questions

- **How accurate is automatic name tagging?** Unknown until the script runs on Acts. Measure misses and double matches before building the UI around it.
- **Does TIPNR's family data cover enough** for useful "Connected to" chips without AI? Check after import.
- **Where should episode summaries come from**: AI per episode, or simpler section headings? Try AI on Acts and judge.
- **Rate limiting and cost caps** for AI calls: resolved by the daily generation cap (Revision 3); generation stays available signed out.
- **Theographic share-alike:** fine for a personal app; revisit before publishing derived data.

## Success Criteria

- Reading Acts 6-13 on the iPhone, every Saul, Philip, Herod, James and John resolves to the right person, with zero confusions on Martin's own test list (see Assignment).
- The person card is served in under 300 ms (server-side p95, warm; see Revision 3) and is readable in about 5 seconds, one-handed.
- Stop reading on the iPhone, open the laptop, and land on the same verse.
- No identity in the app comes from AI. Everything AI-generated is labeled.

## Distribution Plan

Web app deployed on Vercel (or similar), installed to the iPhone home screen. Deploys automatically on push to `main` once production deploys are turned on (Revision 3). No App Store.
